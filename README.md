# FotMob Draft League

A fotmob.com-style page for our FPL Draft league (#23793): table with form, fixtures with odds,
top gameweeks, transfer stats, original squads and each manager's Premier League twin.

## What's on it

The site follows fotmob.com: a home page, then a page for the draft league, each team, each
match, the Premier League and each player, with tabs where fotmob.com has them. Links are
addresses (`#/team/123`, `#/match/9/123-456`, `#/player/351`), so the back button works and any
page can be shared. The old tab links (`#table`, `#squads/123`) still land in the right place.

- **Home**: like fotmob.com's front page. In the middle the round, with arrows and a picker for any gameweek (the current one says so; while it's under way, in green, that it's live, with how many of its Premier League matches are played and how many are on, or when the next kicks off): the draft league's head-to-heads (a softly blinking dot by the gameweek while it's under way, between its matches too; each one's chip says Live while one of its players is on, and between match days, in the same green, when the next kicks off: the time today, the short day otherwise; once its matches are over FT, or FT* until FPL has checked the gameweek's stats, as the bonus, defensive contributions and contentious goals can still change till then) with 1 X 2 odds (live during a round, with in-play odds), then the real Premier League matches day by day, each row with fotmob.com's status chip at its left (FT, or Live or the minute). On the left the leagues, with the team you follow under them (or a link to all the teams and how to follow one), and the prize for the highest scoring gameweek (each score leading to its match); on the right the next deadline, the waivers' or the gameweek's own, whichever comes first, the table (while a gameweek is played the live table, a dot by each team for how its match stands, as fotmob.com's, the score and who against in its tip), the recent transfers (this gameweek's big names first) and Manager of the Month. Until the left column comes in (it does from 1120px wide, as fotmob.com's), the prize sits after the table and the leagues are left to the top and bottom bars. On a phone the right column goes under the round, and a bottom bar leads to the draft league, the Premier League, the Transfer center and the teams.

Each comes with its minute, its match and score, the points it was worth, roughly when it happened in your own time, and the head-to-head's score after it. While a match is on it keeps up with the live relay. An automatic substitute comes in when the substitution is made (once his starter is sure not to play, at the full time of his club's last match or the kick-off of one he isn't in the squad for), with what he'd scored by then, and a chip adds the benches, whose points don't count.
  - **Lineup**: both XIs on one pitch, as on fotmob.com (side by side, or one above the other on a phone; green in the light theme, grey in the dark), with points (bonus shown as 10+3, coloured as fotmob.com's ratings: red for none, orange up to 5, green from 6, and blue for the best of both XIs once the match is over, on a tie the one with more goals, then the higher BPS, then more assists, as for the best of both clubs on a Premier League match's; hovering a player's points takes them apart by where they came from (playing, goals, assists, clean sheet, defensive contribution, saves, bonus, cards and the like), and while his gameweek is played says where they're heading, as the live odds see it, and under Still to come what he can still add, taken apart the same way (the point at 60 minutes, his goals, assists and the rest, the clean sheet kept or lost, a defensive contribution still to reach, the bonus from the BPS race, or from the bench his chance of coming on; parts under 0.05 left out); the same tip wherever a player's points show during a live gameweek, on his page, his team's and a Premier League match's), what each player did (goals, assists, cards, penalties saved and missed, own goals, coming on and going off with the minute, as fotmob.com's icons around the photo), auto-subs, DGW flags and the minute during a round, and the benches below. ## How it works

```
FPL Draft API ──► scripts/build-data.ts ──► public/data.json ──► GitHub Pages
                  (GitHub Actions, started by the Worker's timer: hourly,
                   every 10 min while matches are on, and after transfers)
```

The browser can't call the FPL API itself (no CORS), so a GitHub Actions job builds `data.json`
and the page is a static site in `public/`. Besides the FPL Draft API it reads the main FPL game's
bootstrap and fixtures, and the Premier League's match centre for each match's formations (once per
match, from two hours before kick-off; later builds keep them) and what happened in it when (at every
build while it's on, and once after full time). While it's open, the page re-checks `data.json` every
2 minutes during a live gameweek or while a build for a new transfer is on its way, and every
10 minutes otherwise.

The job is started by the Worker's timer on Cloudflare (`worker/timer.ts`), because GitHub's own
schedule runs late, skips runs and pauses after 60 days without commits; a GitHub schedule every
3 hours stays as a fallback. The 10-minute runs follow the usual Premier League slots (Saturday,
Sunday and weekday evenings, UTC, in both BST and GMT). Games at odd times, like Boxing Day, still
get the hourly run. In between, every 10 minutes the timer compares FPL's owners with the live
`data.json`'s and starts a build if a transfer has gone through, so the projected XIs and the odds
follow a move within about 10 minutes rather than an hour. That matters most just before a
deadline, when the odds freeze.

`data.json` isn't kept in git. Each run starts from the copy on the live site (for the pre-match
odds it froze), builds a new one and deploys only if something changed.

When a season's last gameweek finishes, the build freezes it into `public/seasons/2026-27.json` and
lists it in `public/seasons/index.json`; that archive is the one data file the job commits. For next season, point `LEAGUE_ID` at the new league and the old one
stays browsable from the season picker. `--archive` freezes the current season by hand.

## Live scores (optional, free)

Live scores already update with `data.json`, every 10 minutes during matches. For near
real-time, the page can poll a tiny Cloudflare Worker (`worker/`) that relays the FPL live
feed, which browsers can't call themselves. It answers only these: `/live/<gameweek>`
(the live feed, slimmed from ~450 KB to ~5 KB, with everyone's FPL stats in the matches being
played and their moments from the Premier League's match centre, one fetch per 20 seconds
shared by everyone watching), `/transfers` (this league's moves, trades and owners, refreshed every minute), so
new transfers show up within a couple of minutes instead of at the next build, `/poll/…` (a
head-to-head's Who will win? votes) and `/in-play/<season>/<gameweek>` (each head-to-head's win
chance, recorded every minute while the gameweek's matches are on).
The page, the Worker and the build all score with `src/shared/live.ts`, which adds provisional
bonus and provisional auto-subs.

Its timer fires every minute. Each minute it asks FPL Draft's game state (kept five minutes, a
few hundred bytes) which gameweek is being played; from its deadline until FPL calls it finished,
it hands the minute to the gameweek's Durable Object (`worker/in-play.ts`, one per gameweek, one
request a minute). FPL's main game, whose fixtures would tell the kick-offs, turns Cloudflare's
addresses away, so the object decides from the relay's own live feed (the one `/live` shares)
whether a Premier League match is on, from kick-off until two and a half hours after; when one
is, it works out each head-to-head's live odds from the site's `data.json` (read at most every
5 minutes) and that feed, with the page's code (`src/shared/in-play.ts`), and keeps them as the
build does. In a minute with nothing on it reads nothing more and writes nothing; once the
gameweek is finished, nothing runs at all. The build's timer keeps its own cadence (every tenth
minute's firing, as above). Each data build copies the
records into `data.json` (the relay's URL is `RELAY_URL` in `src/build/config.ts`); when the relay
can't be reached it records at each build alone, as before. While a gameweek is played, a
head-to-head's page asks the relay for the records since the last build, at most once a minute.

1. Deploy it once, with a free Cloudflare account:

   ```bash
   cd worker
   npx wrangler login
   npx wrangler deploy
   ```

2. Put the URL it prints (like `https://fpl-draft-live.<you>.workers.dev`) in `LIVE_URL` in `src/app/state.ts` and push. (This league's relay runs at `https://fpl-draft-live.digernes.workers.dev`.)
3. For the timer that starts the data build: create a fine-grained GitHub token (Settings →
   Developer settings → Fine-grained tokens) for just this repository, with **Actions: Read and
   write**, and store it in the Worker:

   ```bash
   npx wrangler secret put GITHUB_TOKEN
   ```

   Set `GITHUB_REPO` in `worker/wrangler.toml` if your repo has another name. Without the token,
   only GitHub's 3-hourly fallback builds the data.

4. To have it deploy itself with `main`, as the site does: create a Cloudflare API token from the
   **Edit Cloudflare Workers** template (My Profile → API Tokens), and add it and your account ID
   (on the Workers overview) as the repository secrets `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and
   `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` (Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions). The "Deploy the Worker"
   workflow then checks and deploys it whenever a push to `main` changes `worker/` or the shared
   code it bundles; without the secrets it says so and skips. A deploy keeps the Worker's own
   `GITHUB_TOKEN`.

The free plan allows 100,000 requests a day; a busy matchday here uses about ten thousand (the
timer's 1,440 among them), and its Durable Objects a few thousand requests and some hundreds of
rows written, against 100,000 of each a day. The timer's own work fits the free plan's 10 ms of
CPU; the recording itself (about 5 to 10 ms) runs in the Durable Object, which has 30 seconds.

## Run locally

Needs Node 24 or later (it runs the TypeScript build scripts directly).

```bash
npm install
npm run data -- --cache .cache
npm run dev
```

Then open http://localhost:8765. `npm run data` builds `public/data.json`; `--cache .cache` keeps the
raw API responses so later runs don't hit the API. `npm run dev` bundles the page with esbuild,
rebuilds it on every change and serves `public/`.

To try the live views on a day without matches, `node scripts/fake-live.ts` turns the latest
gameweek into one in progress; open http://localhost:8765/?relay=off#/ (so the live relay
doesn't replace it with the real scores), and `node scripts/fake-live.ts --restore` afterwards.

## Code

TypeScript throughout, in strict mode. Node 24 runs the build scripts as they are; esbuild bundles
the page, its styles and the service worker; Wrangler bundles the Worker. CLAUDE.md has the working notes.

```
scripts/build-data.ts   builds data.json (entry point)
scripts/build-site.ts   bundles src/app (script and styles) and src/sw.ts into public/
scripts/fake-live.ts    fakes a gameweek in progress, to try the live views
scripts/snapshot.ts     renders every page from this checkout and from main, to show a change left the site as it was
src/build/              the build, one module per part of data.json
src/app/                the page: its pages in src/app/pages/, the draft league's tabs in src/app/views/,
                        its styles in src/app/styles/, one file per area
src/shared/             data.json's types, the FPL API's Zod schemas, live scoring
src/sw.ts               the service worker
worker/                 the live relay
public/                 the site as deployed: HTML, icons, data.json, seasons/ and the bundles
tests/                  node --test
```

Every FPL API response is parsed with Zod (`src/shared/fpl-api.ts`) before the build or the
Worker uses it, so if FPL changes shape the build stops with the field that broke instead of
writing wrong numbers.

```bash
npm run check    # type-check, lint (ESLint + Prettier) and test
npm run format   # fix formatting and lint
```

CI runs `npm run check` on every push and pull request; scheduled data runs skip it.

## Put it online (free)

1. Create a GitHub repo and push this folder to `main`. Public is simplest: everything in it comes from the public API.
2. In the repo: Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source: **GitHub Actions**.
3. Push, or run Actions → **Update data and deploy** → Run workflow. It checks the code, builds the data, bundles the page and deploys `public/`.
4. The site is at `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`. After that it refreshes itself on the schedule above and only redeploys when the data changed.

Public repos get unlimited Actions minutes, so this costs nothing. Pages on a private repo needs a
paid GitHub plan (and the site is still public to anyone with the link), and the schedule uses
roughly 1,500 of the 2,000 free minutes a month before deploys, so trim the match windows if you go
private. Any other static host (Cloudflare Pages, Netlify) works too, as long as the Action keeps
`public/data.json` fresh.

## How the numbers are made

- **Top gameweeks and the highest-gameweek prize**: the score minus 4 for every player in the scoring XI (after auto-subs) whose club has two fixtures that gameweek. The best adjusted score holds the prize; on a tie the earlier week wins, and two managers level in the same week share it. Only finished gameweeks count. The list gives every manager's best week a place and fills the rest with the highest remaining scores; weeks only there because of that get a "Best GW" tag.
- **Pre-match odds**: when a match starts, the build keeps the odds from its last run before the deadline. Matches that started before this existed (GW1–7 of 2026/27) show what the model would have priced with only the gameweeks before them (from each side's squad that week, without the injury flags of the time), and say so.
- **Odds**: a manager's expected score is the league average plus how far their best projected XI that gameweek sits from the league's average one (by expected points, below), from today's squads, for each of the next three gameweeks. Past scores don't come into it: this season, a manager's form has said nothing about their next score. FPL's injury flags count for the next gameweek. By a later one a doubt has cleared, and an injured or banned player is back if FPL's news gives a return date before its deadline. The spread comes from how far this season's scores land from each week's average (at least 13 points). A draw is both sides landing on the same integer. Prices carry a 5% margin and are rounded down: fractional onto the standard UK ladder, filled in from 1/2 to 4/1 with the in-between fractions some books use (3/4, 20/21, 7/5, 85/40, 13/5…) and with every whole price from 10/1 to 100/1 added (so 46/1 rather than 40/1), then the books' long prices up to 5000/1 for a side all but beaten late in a gameweek (and a sure thing, what shows as 100%, at 1.00 or 1/5000), decimal onto an exchange-style one (steps of 0.01 up to 2.00, wider beyond, up to 5000), which is finer still: 10/11 covers every chance from 48.8% to 49.9%.
- **Luck**: every week is played against the league average, and scoring above it is a win. Beating the league average is the same as beating everyone else's average. Luck is your real table points minus those points: what the fixture list gave or took.
- **Live scores**: each scoring XI's live points with the bonus as it stands: FPL's own live bonus once it has one, or else the top three BPS in a match get 3, 2 and 1 until the real bonus is in (ties share). A starter sure not to play makes way for the first bench player who played and keeps the formation legal, as FPL does at the end of the gameweek: sure once all his club's matches are over without him getting on, or once the Premier League has named the teams and he isn't in his club's squad for those still to come. A bench player who may still play holds the queue, and one left out of his club's squad is passed over. Replaying GW1–7 this way reproduces every official score and all 30 automatic substitutions; `tests/live.test.ts` keeps it that way. The live table ranks everyone as if the live scores were final.
- **Expected points (xP)**: the site's own model (`src/model`, whose README has the details). A player's expected points start from his minutes, from what he played in his club's last ten matches (a match he missed counting less, and a part of one spread about ten minutes either way, since whether he next reaches the hour is no surer than that), scaled by FPL's injury flag. Multiply by his share of his club's attack (from his xG and xA) and by the goals his club can expect against this opponent (a Poisson model of every club's attack and defence, mostly from xG). Both start from last season: his share from his own at the same club, a club's ratings most of the way from last season's (a signing and a promoted club start from the averages), and this season's matches take over as they come. On top come clean sheets, goals conceded and saves from the goals his club can expect to concede, defensive contributions from the chance his tackles, clearances, blocks and interceptions (and recoveries, outside defence) reach FPL's threshold, and cards at his rate. Bonus comes from what his goals, assists, clean sheets and the rest have earned in it at his position this season, plus his own beyond them, and each match's is shared out to the bonus a match hands out (3, 2 and 1). A club's penalties (about 0.12 a match, more for a stronger attack) go to its takers in FPL's order, worth the goal 79% of the time and −2 when missed; a keeper gets 5 for the ones he saves. Each rate is pulled towards his position's average by a few matches' worth. A flagged player's minutes go to his stand-ins, the teammates in his position most likely to come in. Hovering an xP takes it apart: his expected minutes and who against, then the points from playing, goals, penalties, assists, a clean sheet, goals conceded, defensive contributions, saves, bonus and cards. Tested on GW 3–7, its error per player and gameweek was 15% below FPL's own expected points (RMSE 3.28 against 3.88 over the league's squads). FPL's are a player's form (his average over the last 30 days' gameweeks) times his chance of playing; they stand in until the season's first gameweek has been played.
- **Live odds**: while a match is being played, each side's projected final score is its live score plus what each of its XI can still add, from where his match stands:
  - Still to kick off: his expected points for the gameweek. Once his club names its team (about an hour before), a starter's are a starter's: his expected points given he plays, but for at least 75 minutes (a keeper the whole match) and with at least an 86% chance of the hour, as this season's named starters have had, since his own expected minutes count his appearances from the bench too. A substitute's are his chance of coming on by his position (a forward 75%, a midfielder 60%, a defender 40%, a keeper 2%) for 1.3 points, with his manager's bench player counted the rest of the time. One left out has none, and that bench player counts in full.
  - On the pitch: his goals, assists and the rest at his expected rate a minute, for the time he's likely to stay on. Goals come more often late in a match (56% after half-time), so the last half-hour holds 7/18 of them rather than a third. Appearance points and clean sheets arrive at 1 and 60 minutes, so those are worked out from where it stands: the chance he reaches 60 minutes, and of keeping a clean sheet (or still getting one) to the end, with none once a goal has gone in. Defensive contributions are counted from those he has made, from the match's live stats: nothing more once he has reached FPL's threshold (the 2 points are in his score), otherwise the chance he still gets there in the time he's likely still on, at the rate of them his expected points for them stand for (they come about evenly through a match, not late as goals do). All three start at kick-off from his expected points given he plays, so a starter's projection doesn't move when the match starts or when FPL adds the points at 60 minutes. His bonus comes from the BPS race: his chance of finishing first, second or third, from his gap to each of the others and how far their BPS and his can still move (one player's varies by about 12 from one full match to the next, this season, less as the match runs down), level players sharing a place as FPL's do. For the time left he's nudged towards his usual bonus, so at kick-off it's that.
  - Not on the pitch while his match is on: the chance he still comes on, fading from half an hour before the end. One who has gone off has nothing more to come, but the others in the BPS race can still take his bonus.
  - The minute comes from the players' minutes, since FPL leaves the match's own at 0 while it's on. In a double gameweek his expected points are split between his two matches.
  - A starter who won't play (no minutes once his matches are over, left out of his club's squad, or ruled out by FPL as injured, suspended or unavailable) counts the bench player FPL's auto-sub rules would bring on, before the live score has him in; one who may not play (on his club's bench, not on the pitch with his match on, or doubtful before his club has named its team) counts that player by the chance he won't.
  - Only a change in someone's chance of playing moves the expected points while the gameweek is played, since the model learns from finished gameweeks only; FPL's, standing in before the first gameweek, are kept as they were at the deadline, since its form drops then.
  - A player's projection is taken apart in its tip by where what he can still add would come from, adding up to it: the appearance points still to come, his rate a minute shared among his goals, assists, penalties, goals conceded, defensive contributions, saves and cards as his expected points for each have it (what they don't account for, expected points coming in tenths and their parts in hundredths, shared out by their size), the clean sheet he can still keep (or lose, once FPL counts it), the defensive contribution still to reach and the bonus from the BPS race. Before kick-off it's his expected points' parts (a named starter's as a starter's), for a substitute or one not on the pitch his chance of coming on, and one gone off has only his bonus left to lose. FPL's expected points come whole, so with those it stays one figure.
  - Each player still to play adds uncertainty (a spread of 3 points over a full match), so the odds firm up as the games finish. A clean sheet at stake adds more, since it's all or nothing: one goal against costs every one of the club's players on the pitch their clean sheet (and a keeper or defender his provisional bonus) at once, so a lead resting on one club's defence late in a round is less safe than its points look. Same margin and ladders as the pre-match odds.
  - The points still to come aren't spread evenly either way: most of the time a player adds a point or two, now and then he hauls, and only now and then does he lose any, all at once. So each side's final score is a gamma above a floor, what it will almost surely still have, with its drops laid over it. The floor is its points so far: each of its XI keeps what he has (his clean sheet and bonus as they stand, and his goals against as FPL counts them), one yet to play adds nothing should he not play and a point should he, and one gone off can lose only the bonus others are likely to overtake. The drops are each a chance of losing points at once, independent of each other: each player's cards (a yellow, 17.8% over a full match, or a red, 0.8%; one already booked can only be sent off, for 2 more, as FPL scores a second yellow) and an own goal (0.6%), for what's left of his match and by the chance he plays, as this season's 296 yellows, 14 reds and 10 own goals in about 1,670 matches' worth of players' minutes have been, at FPL's −1, −3 and −2 (one yet to play has his point for playing with any of them, so a yellow costs him nothing and a red 2); and each club's clean sheet at stake, all of it lost to one goal against. The gamma has the projection's mean and spread less the drops' (so the side's are as before): skewed right, more so the nearer the mean is to the floor, and close to the normal it replaced while plenty is left. The drops' outcomes are added up exactly (those under one in a billion left out, which keeps them to a few dozen however many players are still to play) and laid over the gamma. Each side's score is on a grid of tenths of a point, and the two are compared; FPL's points are whole, so a draw is the two within half a point. So a side all done on 39 against one on 43 with two of its attackers still to play, one on the pitch with his point in, needs the other to lose 4 points, a red card and an own goal or the like: 1 in 15,000 or less for the draw and less again for the win, rather than the 1 in a few hundred of a floor at the worst case. A lead bigger than the other side can lose is certain. Working the odds out takes a quarter of a millisecond at most.
  - Replayed over the gameweeks so far, between kick-offs and at half-time, these call the head-to-heads about as well as the plain share of each player's expected points did. `scripts/live-odds-check.ts` replays GW 1–7 after each kick-off slot (and 30, 60 and 80 minutes into it) with the model's expected points before each gameweek, as the win chance rebuilds them: the skewed scores give each player's points better chances than the normal did (log loss 2.69 → 2.64), and call the results within the noise of 28 head-to-heads (log loss 0.416 → 0.419, Brier 0.260 → 0.263), none of them drawn, so the 1–5% band of the calibration (mostly the draw with full XIs to play, 2.9%) sees next to none. The rebuild knows nothing of positions, so a clean sheet lost or goals against late put a side below its floor more often there than in the live odds, which have the clean sheet as a drop: each side's final score gets slightly worse chances than with the old floor at the worst case (3.05 → 3.10 with under four players left).
- **Who is still to play**: in a live gameweek each player shows his match minute (in green with a softly blinking dot while he's on the pitch, in grey while his match goes on without him, and his own minutes once he has gone off), FT, or his kick-off time, with the opponent in its tip.
- **Coming on and going off**: FPL doesn't list substitutions, so they come from the minutes in each match. A player who played in more matches than he started came on; a starter with under 90 minutes in a finished match went off, unless he was sent off. While a match is on, everyone still on the pitch has its minute, so a starter more than a minute behind them has gone off; one sent off, starter or substitute, is off the pitch as soon as FPL has the red card, with nothing more to come. A sub came on at the match's minute less his own minutes.
- **Simulated season**: every unfinished match is played 10,000 times with the odds model. Each score is drawn around the manager's expected score with this season's spread, from that gameweek's projected XI, for every gameweek left: today's squads against that week's fixtures (FPL's main game has the whole season's; the Draft game lists only the next few), so a run of easy fixtures, a blank or a double, and a player back from injury on the date FPL's news gives land in the weeks they fall in (one with no return date stays out). A per-run offset (±3 points a week, typically) stands for how good they really are, since squads change and projections are only a guide. Ties on points go to points scored, as in the real table. The random numbers are seeded, so the same data always gives the same percentages and the scheduled job only commits real changes. A match still being played is simulated from scratch, with each side's average over the gameweeks to come.
- **xG table**: each player's points with goals, assists, clean sheets and goals conceded swapped for what their xG, xA and xGC were worth (a clean sheet counts as e^−xGC per full game). Minutes, bonus, saves, cards and defensive contributions stay as scored. xPF adds that up over each scoring XI and xPA does the same for your opponents. xPTS decides every match with a normal model around both expected scores, with the spread of actual minus expected (about 7 points). Sorted by xPTS, as FotMob does. Each finished match shows its own xP and xPTS, which add up to the table, and the lineup filters show each player's share: his xP, xG, xA and xCS (that e^−xGC per full game, for positions where a clean sheet scores).
- **PL twin**: the Premier League club on the same points (or the closest). Managers level on points share out the level clubs in table order. The PL table is rebuilt from the same API's fixtures.
- **Transfer net**: points scored by the player brought in minus the player let go, from that gameweek on. "Banked" is what the new player actually scored in the XI.
- **Original squad**: draft picks still owned today. "% of points" is the share of XI points that came from original picks.
- **Premier League matches**: from the same live feeds as the scores. Each match's players, their minutes and their FPL points come from FPL's breakdown per match (bonus once it is awarded). FPL counts starts for the gameweek, so in a double gameweek a start goes to the match he played longer in, and a side that doesn't come to eleven starters that way gets the eleven with the most minutes. His side is the one the match's BPS list has him on, or his club's. Substitutions come from the minutes, as above. His owner that week is whoever had him in their lineup.
- **Momentum**: the lead over playing time. Each kick-off slot (the matches that start together) takes 90 minutes. Within it the lead moves at each moment that changed the score, at its minute, as the commentary counts them from the match centre's goals, cards and substitutions: the appearance points at kick-off and when a substitute comes on, a goal and its assist, a goal conceded, a card, the hour played, and a clean sheet from the hour while none has gone in, gone again at the first that does while he's on, as FPL's live scores count it (moments within a minute of each other share a mark). What the moments don't show (bonus, saves, defensive contributions and the rest) comes in at the slot's end, the score after its matches (from those same points per match). A slot whose matches' moments aren't known has its end alone, its points coming in evenly over it. The curve is smoothed as fotmob.com's: a moment's change comes in along an S (a Gaussian's, spread 1.5 minutes) over the 9 minutes before it, half of it in the last 4½, so the curve has got there at the moment itself, where its mark is; what comes in evenly (a slot whose moments aren't known, the automatic substitutions) is a straight line with its corners rounded by the same Gaussian. It's drawn through samples every 2 minutes, starts at the first point and ends at the last, and is exactly level wherever nothing changes. Moments less than 9 minutes apart run into each other, so a mark's dot can sit part of the way towards the next. Once the bonus is in, its end is the final score. The x-axis is dotted, as fotmob.com's, and while a match is being played a dot pulses at the end of the curve, the lead as it stands: a match under way (or over since the last build) comes from the live feed, as the score does, its players' points so far less those of their club's matches before it. Each automatic substitute comes in at the moment his substitution is made, as the commentary has it (only one it can't time comes last, in half a slot of their own). While it's played, each comes in when the live scores have made his substitution, as the commentary times it (at the full time of his starter's club's last match, the kick-off of one his starter isn't in the squad for, or his own coming on if that's later), as a moment with its own mark ("Auto-sub: Hill on for Calvert-Lewin, +11"): what he'd scored by then comes in at once, and the rest as he earns it, his moments from then on counting for his side. One made ahead of a kick-off still to come, once the teams are out, comes in where play has got, so the curve ends on the live score.
- **Win chance**: the momentum's other view. Before the first kick-off it is the pre-match odds. At each moment within a slot and after each slot, each side's expected score is its points then plus, for each of its XI, the share of his expected points for the gameweek (those the live odds start from) from what is left of his club's matches, by the minute, with the live odds' spread of 3 points a player for that share of his gameweek, their floor (each keeps what he has, so the side's points then) and their drops, his cards and an own goal for what was left of each of his matches (with his point for playing for one not yet started); the chances come from the two skewed scores as the live odds' do. An automatic substitute counts as the starter he came on for until that starter's matches are over, then as himself, and his points for certain once his own are over; while the gameweek is played, from his moment on the curve he's one of the XI, his points so far in the score. A gameweek before the one being played, for which data.json keeps no player's expected points, shares each side's projected score at the deadline evenly over its XI. While the match is on, the curve ends at the live odds the page shows; with nothing left to play it is the win, the draw or the defeat for certain.
  - What the live odds said: every minute while any of the gameweek's Premier League matches is on (from kick-off until two and a half hours after), the live relay's Worker works out each head-to-head's live odds as the page does (the same code, with the site's expected points and the relay's live feed) and records them with their time and the live score; and at every data build while a head-to-head is being played (every 10 minutes through the usual match slots, hourly otherwise), from the deadline on, the build does too and records them in data.json, after those the last build recorded, which stay once it's over, with the Worker's put in among them: the Worker's take the place of any of the build's within 10 minutes of one, and the build's fill any gap in the Worker's (as before the Worker kept any, or when it can't be reached). A new record takes the latest's place unless either side's chance has moved by half a percentage point since the last kept, so the first and the latest are always there. While the gameweek is played, a head-to-head's page adds the Worker's records since the last build, asked for at most once a minute. In a slot with records, the curve is those, at their time within the kick-off slots, each coming in evenly from the one before: the minute is the time since the slot's kick-off up to 45, 45 through first-half stoppage time and half-time (until 62 minutes after kick-off), then 17 less, up to 90; from 112 minutes until the next slot's kick-off, its end. Those before the first kick-off aren't drawn (the pre-match odds stand there), nor any in the minute before where play has got (the live odds now take their place). A recorded slot's end is the last record after its matches, or the chance worked out again without one. Slots without records (any before this existed) are worked out again as above. Hovering a record says when it was (64’ of the 15:00 kick-offs, half-time, after them), the chances and the score then.
- **Live and FT**: a head-to-head is Live while a club with a player in either squad is playing, and FT once every one of them is done; between match days it counts down to the next kick-off among them.
- **Formations**: the Premier League's match centre lists each XI line by line from the goalkeeper, each line from the right of the team to its left, by the same player codes FPL uses; the pitch turns them to face the right way.
- **Top transfers**: a move of a big name, one of the first 50 picks in the league's draft or of the season's 50 top scorers so far; the gameweek's biggest come first, by whichever of the two ranks him higher.
- **Player stats**: FPL gives most of a player's stats for the whole gameweek, and lists only some match by match (goals, assists, cards, saves, bonus, BPS, defensive contributions and the like). So someone who played twice in a double gameweek has just those for each match, and a dash for the rest. While a match is on (and until a build after full time has it) they all come from the live relay, as fresh as the scores; xG, xA, xP and xCS in a gameweek being played are worked out from them the way the build does once it's over, and so are a match's xP and xCS; the season's are its gameweeks' added up. The season's Projected category looks five gameweeks ahead, about a month of fixtures, beyond which a player's projections barely move; its goals and assists are as many as the points the model expects of them stand for, the penalties he'd take included. The points xP keeps as scored are FPL's own explanation of them once a gameweek is over, and while it's played worked out from his stats as FPL scores them (which gives FPL's own, for every player in a single match this season).
- **Similar players**: the same position, nearest on season points and on expected points for the next gameweek.
- **Match report**: "thrashed" is a win by 25 or more, "edged" one by 3 or fewer. An upset is a winner who had under a 35% chance at the deadline. The winner's top scorer made the difference when he scored more than the margin (while it's on, his share of the leader's points is given instead, from 5 points); a comeback is from 5 points or more behind after any Premier League match; an automatic substitute is mentioned from 5 points, and the loser's bench when the best XI the squad had (the formation kept, in hindsight) would have won. A haul left on a bench is 10 points or more.
- **Manager of the Month**: FPL's own months (its main game's phases; August is GW 1–2, September GW 3–5 …). Manager of the Month has the most table points from the month's head-to-heads, then the most points scored.
- **A player's story**: his owner each week is the manager whose lineup he was in at the deadline. An unclaimed week is one as a free agent with no waiver claim for him. A claim that would have dropped him and didn't go through (the player wanted went to someone with better priority, or was already gone) counts as a near miss if he stayed. If the manager let him go that gameweek anyway (on a claim ranked lower, as a free agent or in a trade), the timeline still tells of a claim lost to someone else, but not of one ranked below the claim that dropped him, which FPL never got to.

## Notes

- The FPL Draft API is unofficial and undocumented. If it changes, the schemas in `src/shared/fpl-api.ts` say where, and `src/build/` is the only place to fix.
- The look follows fotmob.com's colours and layout. The real font (GT Walsheim) is licensed, so the page uses it only if it's installed and otherwise falls back to DM Sans.
- Player photos and club crests are loaded from the Premier League's image servers; nothing comes from FotMob.
